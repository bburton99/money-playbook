The 8 Movesin this order
1

Run your numbers

Give every dollar a job: tithing (10%) off the top on payday — budget it first — then split the rest 50% needs · 30% wants · 20% saving. You can't fix what you don't track.

Do it tonight
Track one month of spending in a free budgeting app
2

Stash $1,000 fast

A starter emergency fund in a high-yield savings account keeps a blown tire from becoming card debt. Grow it to 3–6 months of expenses once you're working full-time.

Do it this week
Open a HYSA at a different bank than your checking
3

Avoid debt. Borrow on purpose.

Don't finance a lifestyle: no revolving balances, minimize loans on depreciating assets. Your car has one job — getting you there. Function over form; the flex can wait. Student loans only where the degree pays for itself. Carrying 20%+ card debt? Avalanche it: highest APR first.

Borrowing rule
Total student debt under your expected first-year salary
4

Build credit like an adult

One no-fee card, autopay the full balance every month, stay under 30% of the limit. Carrying a balance does not build credit — that myth just costs you interest.

Set & forget
Autopay: statement balance, in full, every month
5

Take the free money

Contribute enough to grab every dollar of your 401(k) match — a guaranteed 50–100% return. Check the vesting schedule.

10 minutes
One HR form. Done.
6

Invest boring. Automate it.

Open a Roth IRA (up to $7,500 in 2026), buy low-cost index funds, auto-invest every payday. Time in the market beats timing the market — every time.

Target
15–25% of gross income, automated — the core never gets touched
7

Protect the downside

Health + renters (~$15/mo) + disability insurance now. Life insurance only when someone depends on your income — then cheap 20–30yr term (~10× income). Never whole life.

Red flag
"Advisors" pitching whole life to 22-year-olds work for commission, not you
8

Earn your swings

Moves 1–7 locked in? Now you get a sandbox: 5–10% of your portfolio, max — single stocks, a real estate fund, a side venture. Separate account, never borrowed money, and skim the wins back into index funds. Losses are tuition.

House rule
Only money you could lose 100% of without changing your life

Rules That Never Change
01Pay God and yourself first. Tithing and savings come out on payday — before you can spend them.
02Spend less than you earn — invest the gap. That sentence is 90% of personal finance.
03If it sounds too good to be true, it is. Real wealth compounds slowly, then suddenly.
04Your biggest asset is your earning power. Your degree and skills out-earn any hot stock tip.
